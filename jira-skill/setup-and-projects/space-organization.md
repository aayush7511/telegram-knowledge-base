# Space Organization Models

## Three Proven Organizational Approaches

**Critical Insight**: "There is no one-size fits all approach to structuring a project in Jira." Choose based on your organizational reality.

### Model 1: Team-Based Structure

**Organization**: One space per team/department

**Example**:
- Backend Team → Backend Space
- Frontend Team → Frontend Space
- Marketing Team → Marketing Space
- IT Ops → IT Ops Space

**Advantages**:
- Clear team ownership
- Simplified permission management
- Team autonomy
- Easy accountability

**Best For**:
- Organizations with distinct departmental boundaries
- Limited cross-functional work
- Small to mid-sized organizations

**Challenges**:
- Cross-team visibility harder
- Duplicate workflows across teams
- Collaboration between teams requires coordination

### Model 2: Business Unit Structure

**Organization**: One space per business division

**Example**:
- Marketing Division → Marketing Space
- Engineering Division → Engineering Space
- Finance Division → Finance Space

**Advantages**:
- Consistency within business units
- Standardized workflows per unit
- Clear business unit alignment
- Easier budget tracking

**Best For**:
- Large organizations with distinct divisions
- Organizations with standardized processes per unit
- Government agencies with bureau structure

**Challenges**:
- Cross-division visibility requires special boards
- Different divisions may need different processes

### Model 3: Product-Based Structure

**Organization**: One space per product or product family

**Example**:
- Mobile App v2.0 → Mobile Space
- Payment System → Payments Space
- Analytics Platform → Analytics Space

**Advantages**:
- Clear product ownership
- Release cycle alignment
- Product-focused reporting
- Version and roadmap clarity

**Best For**:
- Software product organizations
- Multiple product teams
- Organizations leveraging Jira's release features

**Challenges**:
- Team members span multiple spaces
- Permission management complexity
- Shared resource allocation challenges

## Choosing Your Model

**Questions to Ask**:

1. **How is your organization structured?** (Teams, Divisions, Products?)
2. **Do you have cross-functional work?** (Teams, Divisions, or Products?)
3. **What's your primary reporting need?** (Team performance, Business unit success, Product delivery?)
4. **Do you need centralized governance?** (Yes = Centralize more, No = Team autonomy)

**Recommendation for Government**:
- Use **Business Unit or Team-Based** model
- Ensures clear accountability boundaries
- Facilitates audit trail separation
- Supports compliance compartmentalization

## Space Configuration Best Practices

1. **Clear Naming**: Use descriptive space names (not "Project A")
2. **Documentation**: Document your organizational model and rationale
3. **Consistency**: Apply chosen model consistently across organization
4. **Flexibility**: Allow for evolution as organization grows
5. **Governance**: Define who can create new spaces (centralized for company-managed)

## Evolution & Scaling

Your organizational model can evolve:
- **Start small**: Begin with one space per team
- **Standardize**: As you grow, enforce consistency
- **Refactor**: Reorganize spaces as business changes (quarterly/annually)
- **Document changes**: Track rationale for audits
